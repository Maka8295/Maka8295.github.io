Lucca 58HE

hall effect · split · 58 keys · daily driver

A fifty-eight key split keyboard in which no key is a switch at all. Beneath each cap sits a magnet and beneath the board a DRV5053 hall sensor, read through an ADG732 32-channel multiplexer into the RP2040's ADC. Key travel resolves to about 7 µm, so rapid trigger and an actuation point anywhere from 0 to 3.5 mm are a matter of firmware.

mcu
Waveshare RP2040-Zero, one per half
sense
DRV5053 hall sensors via ADG732 mux
light
SK6803 Mini-E per-key RGB, 10-point underglow
link
USB-C; SPI or UART between halves
extra
SSD1306 OLED, 3D-printed case, KMK firmware

Brought up under KMK and CircuitPython. This page was typed on it.

FT2232H Breakout

debug tool · JTAG / SPI / I2C / UART · level shifted

Two channels of FTDI's FT2232H, each passed through an SN74LVC8T245 level shifter so the target side can run at 1.8, 2.5, 3.3 or 5 V at the flick of a DIP switch. A hardware isolation switch detaches a chip clip from its target without pulling a wire, and switchable 4.7 kΩ pull-ups make I2C work on either channel.

power
LDK130 1.8 / 2.5 V, AP2112K 3.3 V
esd
USBLC6-2 on USB

Flumper

flash dumper · 0.1 → 0.3 · KAGE Engineering

A USB tool for reading the flash and EEPROM memories of other devices. Edition 0.1 carried an FT232H and taught hard lessons about USB high speed: the TVS diodes were too capacitive, the differential pair was sloppy, and a misnamed net left the multiplexers unpowered. Edition 0.2 moved to the dual-channel FT2232H, added level shifting from 1.8 to 5 V and sixteen spare GPIO, and matched the USB pair to 90 Ω over unbroken ground. Edition 0.3, above, shrinks onto an FT4222H and pushes the target memory out onto a socketed cartridge, so a burned chip is swapped rather than mourned.

editions
0.1 FT232H · 0.2 FT2232H · 0.3 FT4222HQ
shift
TMUX1574 switches, 1.8 to 5 V targets
targets
SPI flash, I2C EEPROM, via memcart
power
LDK130 1.8 V, AP2112K 3.3 V

memcart carries the chip and three solder jumpers; flumpcable reaches a footprint still soldered to its board.

Badge 2026

TENGUCON 3.0 · Akihabara · wearable

A wearable computer for a Tokyo hacker gathering. An ESP32-S3 module drives a display over SPI, a Ra-01S LoRa radio for chatting badge to badge, a microSD card on the SDMMC bus, and an infrared blaster with a TSOP38238 receiver for learning and replaying remote codes. Below the screen lies a 58-key matrix of low-profile tactile switches, so the badge is also a keyboard, and ten addressable LEDs light its edges.

ESP32-S3-WROOM-2
radio
Ra-01S LoRa, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth
input
58-key matrix, 1N4148 diodes, 74HC4051
ir
TSOP38238 receiver, 940 nm emitters
store
microSD over SDMMC

Firmware in Rust on ESP-IDF: LoRa chat, IR blaster that reads Flipper Zero files, SD explorer, ELF app loader.

Shuriken 2

bench controller · USB hub · switched power

A multifunction controller for KAGE's test benches. An RP2040 commands a USB2514B four-port hub, so one cable from the host fans out to four USB-C ports, each with its own power switch. Barrel-jack inputs feed TPS56A37 buck converters, and AO4407A high-side switches, polyfuses and TVS diodes let the board power-cycle a device under test without a hand ever reaching for the plug.

mcu
RP2040, W25Q128 flash
hub
Microchip USB2514B, 4× USB-C
power
TPS56A37 bucks, AO4407A switched outputs
protect
7 A polyfuses, SMBJ TVS, USBLC6-2
store
microSD slot

Its forebear ran an STM32G474 with CAN, RS485 and relays. The second trims that to what the benches use.

Lucca58

mechanical · hot-swap · two layouts

The mechanical sibling of the 58HE: the same fifty-eight key split footprint, but with hot-swap sockets for ordinary switches in place of hall sensors, which makes it a far simpler and cheaper board. Two layouts were drawn, one columnar and one row-staggered, and both carry production files ready for JLCPCB.

Lazy Kiwi

ESP32-S3 · PCB antenna · drop-in wireless

A compact ESP32-S3 board with its antenna etched into the copper, external PSRAM and flash, and a USB-C port wired straight to the chip's native USB. Boot and reset buttons sit on the board, and the whole is meant to drop into larger projects as their wireless part.

SFFSFC

Super Famicom · 4 layers · HDMI + TOSLINK · in progress

A small-form-factor re-engineering of the Super Famicom's revision 2 motherboard. The original two layers are being redrawn on four, with HDMI video and TOSLINK audio brought out directly, so a stock chipset can drive a modern display without a scaler between. The work stands at schematic capture.
